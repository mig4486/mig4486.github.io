Privacy Policy

Last updated November 25, 2026.

This policy applies to MarxTone for iPhone and iPad, its pages on this site, and requests you send to support. For questions about privacy, access, correction, or deletion, contact the developer at tgstartup@icloud.com.

Personal library on your device

When you import audio, MarxTone reads the files you select in the system picker and saves local copies. App data can include track names, artists, artwork, indexes of selected folders, playlists, ratings, your queue, playback position, and settings. MarxTone uses these data to display your collection, play music, and restore your state. Local playback works without an app account. Your personal library is stored on your device and in any backup or cloud location you choose.

Track ratings and recent plays help recommend catalog music on your device. You can clear listening history in My Music. Catalog recommendations use ratings and listening history for catalog tracks.

Lyrics from an .lrc file you choose are stored on the device for display with the track. You can replace or remove them on the Lyrics screen. The contents remain separate from analytics and manual library exports.

Imported audio, lyrics, the database, and settings may be included in a device backup or transfer to another iPhone or iPad, depending on system settings and your choices. MarxTone excludes downloaded catalog and cloud audio, incomplete downloads, and cached artwork from system backups. You may need to download those tracks again after a transfer.

When exporting manually, you choose where to save the file. A library backup can contain tracks, playlists, the queue, and playback position, and some export types include recovery history. The backup and the separately exported diagnostic report are unencrypted files. A diagnostic report is shared only if you choose to send it.

Cloud connections

The sources available depend on your version and the services available on the device. If you connect a service directly, that provider handles sign-in and grants read access. MarxTone uses session data, an account identifier, and available account details to maintain the connection. For folders you choose, MarxTone reads track details and audio to show and refresh the list, play tracks, or save available offline copies.

  • Google Drive. A direct connection uses drive.readonly, which can read files available to the signed-in account. MarxTone works with the folder you choose and its accessible subfolders. Use and transfer of Google API data comply with the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Google API Services User Data Policy.
  • OneDrive. Delegated Files.Read access allows MarxTone to read files for the signed-in user. Microsoft Privacy Statement.
  • Dropbox. Read permissions for account information, metadata, and file content let you choose a folder and play your music. Dropbox Privacy Policy.
  • iCloud Drive and Files. The system picker grants access to the files or folder you select through the operating system and file provider. Apple Privacy Policy.

Requests for personal files go from your device to the provider you select. Account and session data operate the connection. File details operate your library. Personal audio, cloud account information, and data obtained through Google APIs are excluded from product analytics and advertising.

Online catalog and website

MarxTone connects to catalog publishers and file hosts for updates, artwork, streaming, and downloads. Those services can receive your IP address, request time, requested resource, and technical headers. The track details show the source when it is available. File hosts handle these requests under their own policies.

GitHub Pages hosts the MarxTone website and processes technical visit data. These pages contain no analytics scripts, forms, or ads. GitHub Privacy Statement.

Some catalog tracks link to lyrics on a publisher's website. MarxTone matches the link to the catalog track on your device. When you open a link, the system browser connects to the publisher, which receives standard visit data. The lookup uses the public catalog track details.

Advertising and subscriptions are disabled in this version.

On-device analytics journal

The journal is off by default. In Settings, open Local Analytics to allow local logging of categories such as navigation, playback, downloads, or errors. The journal stores action, source, and outcome categories, elapsed-time ranges, a random event number, and the UTC date. It stays on the device.

MarxTone keeps up to 2,048 recent events from the last 30 UTC calendar days in the app cache. It checks retention while running and on the next launch. Clearing the app cache can remove the journal and reset consent.

You can clear the journal and keep your preference, or turn it off and clear its entries. Playback, downloads, and recommendations are available with either choice.

Google Analytics and crash reporting

In Settings under Local Analytics, you can separately allow data sharing with Google Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics. Both switches are off on first install. You can use the on-device journal independently.

Google Analytics helps the developer understand which features are used and where errors occur. Google processes usage categories, app version and device information, an app-instance identifier, and technical session data. Product events exclude search queries, personal track names, file paths, and cloud sign-in information. Advertising features of the SDK are disabled.

Crashlytics helps diagnose crashes. Google receives device and app version information, installation identifiers, and crash details such as stack traces. If analytics is also enabled, a report may include events leading up to the crash. For separately reported Dart errors, MarxTone sends a diagnostic category and code locations, excluding the original error message.

You can withdraw either consent in Settings. Turning off Google Analytics resets its data on the device. Turning off Crashlytics requests deletion of reports awaiting upload. Contact tgstartup@icloud.com for requests about data already received. Google's Firebase privacy documentation explains its processing and retention practices.

Storage, disconnection, and removal

  • Disconnect a cloud source in MarxTone to stop using the connection. You can also revoke access in the provider's settings. Local copies and related track details may remain.
  • Use Storage to remove downloaded copies. Deleting a copy in MarxTone leaves the original with the provider.
  • Device backups and manually exported files may remain after you remove app data. Manage them in system settings or in the location you chose for export.

Local files and settings remain while needed for the features you use or until you remove them. The app or operating system may delete artwork, partial downloads, and other temporary data. Use your cloud provider's settings to manage files held by that provider.

Network transfers use HTTPS. Access to a copy saved outside the player depends on the location you choose and its permissions.

Support correspondence and data requests

When you contact support, the developer receives your email address, message, and any attachments you send. This information is used to respond, diagnose a problem, or review a rights notice. Correspondence is retained while needed to handle your request and meet applicable obligations.

To request access, correction, or deletion of information you sent, email tgstartup@icloud.com and identify the request. For data held by Apple or another provider, use that provider's account controls or support.
